Clinical Guideline: Evaluation and Management of Pneumonia
1. Diagnostic Triggers (Adults)
Initial Suspicion: Acute onset of fever/chills and cough (productive or non-productive).

Risk Probability: Baseline probability of pneumonia in acute cough is 5%. If all vital signs are normal, probability drops to 1%.

Chest Radiograph (CXR) Indications:

Vital Signs: Temp >100°F (37.8°C), HR >100 bpm, or RR >20 bpm.

Physical Exam: Crackles or decreased breath sounds (in patients without asthma).

Guideline Standard: IDSA 2016 recommends CXR with demonstrable infiltrate to confirm diagnosis and exclude acute bronchitis.

Delayed Presentation: 1 in 10 patients with a clinical diagnosis of CAP but a normal initial CXR will show radiographic evidence within 72 hours. If clinical suspicion is high, treat empirically and repeat CXR in 24–48 hours.

2. Adult Severity Assessment & Disposition
CURB-65 Tool: (1 point each for Confusion, Urea >7 mmol/L, RR ≥30, BP <90/60, Age ≥65).

Score 0–1: Low risk; Outpatient care.

Score 2: Moderate risk; Consider short-stay inpatient or close monitoring.

Score 3+: High risk; Inpatient admission (consider ICU for 4-5).

CRB-65: Alternative for primary care (excludes Urea).

3. Pediatric Considerations
Diagnosis: Fever, cough, and tachypnea are primary indicators.

Etiology by Age:

0–5 years: Primarily viral (RSV, Influenza). Bacterial causes include S. pneumoniae and H. influenzae.

>5 years: Bacterial causes become more common (M. pneumoniae, S. pneumoniae).

Treatment: Amoxicillin remains first-line for outpatient bacterial pneumonia. Macrolides are used for suspected atypical pathogens (more common in children >5 yrs).

4. Empirical Treatment Summary (Adult CAP)
Outpatient (Healthy): Macrolide (Azithromycin) or Doxycycline.

Outpatient (Comorbidities): Respiratory Fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin/Moxifloxacin) OR Beta-lactam plus Macrolide.

Inpatient (Non-ICU): Respiratory Fluoroquinolone OR Beta-lactam plus Macrolide.

5. Key Pathogen Risk Factors
Alcoholism: S. pneumoniae, Anaerobes, K. pneumoniae.

COPD/Smoking: H. influenzae, P. aeruginosa, M. catarrhalis.

Recent Travel: Legionella (hotels/cruises).

Influenza Co-infection: S. aureus (MRSA), S. pneumoniae.

6. When to Expand Testing (Cultures/Urine Antigen)
ICU admission.

Failure of outpatient antibiotic therapy.

Cavitary infiltrates or pleural effusion.

Active alcohol abuse or severe liver disease.

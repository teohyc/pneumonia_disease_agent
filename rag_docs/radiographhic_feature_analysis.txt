================================================================================
PNEUMONIA ETIOLOGY EVALUATION GUIDELINE
================================================================================

1. VIT PREDICTION / IMAGE FEATURE EXTRACTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary Objective: Identify structural patterns of inflammation.

A. BACTERIAL PATTERNS (Alveolar Process)
- Primary Feature: Consolidation (dense, opaque white areas).
- Key Sign: Air bronchograms (dark, air-filled bronchi visible against dense tissue).
- Distribution: Typically focal, lobar, or segmental.

B. VIRAL PATTERNS (Interstitial Process)
- Primary Feature: Ground-Glass Opacities (GGO) (hazy, gray opacity; underlying vessels remain visible).
- Secondary Feature: Peripheral linear opacities or reticular patterns (intersecting lines indicating thickened connective tissue).
- Distribution: Typically bilateral, peripheral, and favoring lower lobes.


2. REASONER 1 & RAG RETRIEVAL: CLINICAL HISTORY & RISK FACTORS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary Objective: Contextualize the image with patient presentation.

A. BACTERIAL PRESENTATION
- Onset: Acute, sudden.
- Symptoms: High fever, shaking chills (rigors), productive cough (purulent: yellow/green/rust sputum), pleuritic chest pain.
- Risk Factors: Advanced age, COPD/asthma, recent influenza infection, immunosuppression.

B. VIRAL PRESENTATION
- Onset: Gradual, subacute.
- Symptoms: Low-grade fever, non-productive (dry) cough, severe myalgias, headache, profound fatigue, upper respiratory symptoms (rhinorrhea).
- Risk Factors: Known exposure, seasonal outbreaks, crowded living conditions.


3. REASONER 2: LAB & BIOMARKER CROSS-VALIDATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary Objective: Evaluate if the retrieved clinical data aligns with the ViT prediction.

A. BACTERIAL BIOMARKERS
- White Blood Cell (WBC): Leukocytosis (Elevated, often >15,000/mcL) with a "left shift".
- Procalcitonin (PCT): High (>0.25 ng/mL to >0.5 ng/mL).
- C-Reactive Protein (CRP): Significantly elevated (often >100 mg/L).
- Microbiology: Positive sputum culture (requires 24-48 hrs).

B. VIRAL BIOMARKERS
- White Blood Cell (WBC): Normal or Leukopenia (Low), often with lymphopenia.
- Procalcitonin (PCT): Low (<0.1 ng/mL).
- C-Reactive Protein (CRP): Normal to moderately elevated.
- Microbiology: Negative bacterial sputum culture; Positive RT-PCR viral panel.

C. DISCREPANCY HANDLING (Routing back to Reasoner 1)
- Trigger: If ViT predicts "Viral" (GGOs) BUT patient has high PCT (>0.25) and purulent sputum.
- Action: RAG must search for "secondary bacterial infection superimposed on viral pneumonia" or "atypical bacterial presentation".


4. EXPLAINER: SYNTHESIS RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary Objective: Generate final diagnostic explanation.

- Rule 1: Never diagnose on imaging alone. Always state the ViT visual findings first, followed by the supporting or contradicting lab/clinical evidence.
- Rule 2: If PCT is high and consolidation is present, weight the explanation heavily toward bacterial etiology.
- Rule 3: If GGOs are present and WBC/PCT are low, weight the explanation heavily toward viral etiology.
- Rule 4: Explicitly mention if the data is insufficient (e.g., missing PCT or sputum cultures) and recommend those tests to the user.
